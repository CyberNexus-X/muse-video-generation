# 02 — Video types and templates

## Two types, and how to tell them apart

### A. Short reel — vertical
- **Size:** 1080x1920, 30 fps
- **Length:** roughly 25–32 seconds
- **Language:** Hinglish (Roman, unless the user supplies Devanagari)
- **Structure:** hook + 3 steps + tip + comment-CTA
- **Goal:** one tool / one idea, saved and shared, comment keyword → DM guide/link
- **Examples of distinct templates (each matched to its own script):**
  - FAST / speed: stopwatch, a time-saved progress bar (e.g. 60 MIN → 10 MIN), a checklist card with rows ticking green one by one
  - QUIZ / exam: exam-paper look
  - PHOTO / gallery: gallery / polaroid fan-out
  - CV / document: professional document look
  - ROUTINE / day plan: day-timeline
  - SHORT / YouTube Shorts: a long video visually splitting into several vertical Shorts; palette white, charcoal-black, YouTube red, amber CTA only
  - Lime-grid toolkit style: light graph-paper grid, white/black/bright lime-green, bold centred statements, fast pop transitions, karaoke subtitles, per-second motion

### B. Long video — landscape
- **Size:** 1920x1080, 30 fps
- **Length:** sample ~50–60 seconds; full video ~9–10 minutes
- **Language:** Hinglish
- **Structure:** 5 scenes (sample) or 8–12 scenes (full), in the order in `docs/01-scripting.md`
- **Goal:** explain one topic properly, with animated diagrams, not a talking head
- **Example style — cream editorial:**
  - Cream canvas + peach wash, black headlines, per-scene accent colours
  - Top-left brand mark (only if the user's own mark) + top-right slide-number pill
  - Heavy clean sans-serif, large headlines/numbers, bold lower-third subtitles in 2–3 short lines
  - Hard cuts between numbered slides, pop/fade-in elements inside slides
  - Highlight-box captions building word by word (key words in a black or accent box, upcoming words muted grey)
  - Counting numbers, stacked cards, pill tags, red X on wrong options, coloured border on the correct card, branching diagrams from a central node
  - Thin progress segments

## The distinct-template rule

**Every video gets its own template, matched to that video's script and frames.**

- Do NOT reuse one shared template across a set of videos and only change the colour.
- Do NOT reuse the same layout, same cards, and same motion for every topic.
- The template must come from the script: a checklist topic gets a checklist; a timeline topic gets a timeline; a gallery topic gets a gallery.
- If the user dislikes a video's look and delegates the new look to you, invent a completely new look for that video. Do not just tweak the old one and call it new.

## Style references — how to use them

1. Watch / inspect the reference reel. Note only its **editing style**: canvas, colours, type, caption style, pacing, transitions, graphics.
2. Write an **original script** on the user's topic.
3. Build **original animation** in that style.
4. Never copy the reference's script, lyrics, audio, brand name, or logo.
5. If you report the style, also report its limits honestly: for example, "closing end-card not verified from sampled frames" or "music unconfirmed, watched muted" — do not guess the parts you did not see.

## What a template is NOT

- A template is not a text-slide deck. Every template needs motion, diagrams, and per-second change.
- A template is not a prompt for an AI video generator. The video is built and rendered here, scene by scene.
